# 3-2-1 Reveal V1

3-2-1 Reveal is a reusable curiosity-to-payoff format for reference-led ecommerce videos. The name does not mean the source must display or say “3, 2, 1.” Its mechanism is the reference's actual progression: concealment or uncertainty, anticipation, reveal, product proof, and payoff. Some sources reveal through a physical cover, some through an edit, and some through an activated result.

## Reference is the blueprint

For reference recreation, preserve the source's observed shots, order, approximate timestamps, actions, opening and ending states, camera movement, environment, editing rhythm, captions, audio direction, and emotional progression. Apply only explicit user changes, scoped to the named subjects, products, or scenes. Keep original observations separate from requested changes. Reuse the existing character, product, scene-continuity, prompt-compilation, revision, and asset-versioning paths.

Do not force a fixed shot count, duration, three anticipation beats, visible countdown, blanket, or physical unveiling. A requested clip count is a preference; retain source scenes and shot boundaries in reference mode. Keep a reveal and its concealment together when they happen in one continuous source shot. If the source uses a hard cut, preserve the cut and show the resulting product use immediately after it.

## Plan each reveal

Record the concealed or uncertain subject; how it is concealed; its visible silhouette, scale, and support; the person performing the reveal and their grip; physical reveal versus edit; when the reveal starts and completes; what is visible immediately afterward; and why this matters to the product story. Use `source_observation` for the unmodified source and `applied_user_changes` only for explicit substitutions. Do not invent a physical reveal when none occurs.

For covered openings, the starting image must show the actual frame-zero state: environment and camera, cover shape/material/size/color, folds and contact, supported object position, hand placement, plausible shadows and occlusion. Keep the target hidden when the source hides it. A product image defines product appearance, not its studio background; a character image defines identity, not the scene or wardrobe. Later starting images show the correct source-specific state.

For motion, describe the ordered action, approximate source timing, physical cause and contact, camera movement, reveal mechanism, required final state, relevant sound, and only the most important failure risks. A physical cover moves from its observed grip, folds naturally, and fully clears the intended area. A cut-based reveal stays a cut. Functional demonstrations and activation show the real observable mechanism and result. Keep packaged, unpacked, installed, inactive, and activated states distinct and in source order when present.

## Continuity and editing

Keep recurring identities and product construction stable across shots while allowing intentional changes such as product variants, new performers, locations, wardrobe, or day-to-night states. Preserve actual source cuts, pacing, reveal timing, caption concepts/placement/timing, and requested native sounds. Do not invent music or dialogue. Keep suspense copy distinct from a later CTA. Use the existing Resolve workflow for edit assembly and export.

Use the shared prompt compiler and safe compaction. Keep final Grok prompts below 3,800 characters while retaining reveal movement, timing, ending state, product proof, explicit changes, continuity, and required sound. Motion-only revisions reuse approved starting images; correct starting-frame geometry or concealment at the image stage. Preserve versions, costs, approvals, and job history. Never run automatic paid retries.

## Canonical reference observations

These examples document source-specific mechanics. They are not runtime assets or mandatory templates.

A gingerbread costume appears in the next shot; later scenes show the tree costume in a festive city and another winter outfit by a window. Preserve the intentional costume variants and locations.
- **Baby Santa — `2026-11-22 16.02.36.mp4`, about 12.7s:** a red patterned blanket covers a seated baby on a snowy deck; an adult raises it around 3.8–4.2s. Follow-up scenes show the baby walking indoors, approaching family outdoors, and two babies sitting together. Preserve the reveal timing, blanket/hand geometry, identity, and intentional second baby.
- **Interactive game — `2026-11-22 16.02.41.mp4`, about 15.5s:** a person holds a white cover before camera, then a hard cut reveals children using a projected game and toy blasters. Later shots show the targets, players, controllers, and game unit; a later caption asks viewers to comment “BLAST.” Preserve the hard cut and credible function; do not create a cloth-removal animation.
- **Snow-removal comparison — `2026-11-22 16.02.44.mp4`, about 18.7s:** side-by-side snow-covered cars, tool demonstrations, POV, and close-ups. This is primarily a Versus reference, not mandatory Reveal choreography.

## Boundaries

Do not copy any example's cast, products, setting, choreography, shot count, duration, trademark, or CTA into unrelated jobs. Keep these notes separate from generic runtime rules. Do not claim deterministic reproduction; generated footage requires review.
